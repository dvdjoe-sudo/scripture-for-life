# Psalms illustration standard

Approved by the user October 19, 2026: soft, detailed, naturalistic watercolor matching the selected Psalm 23:2 quiet-water image. This is the visual standard for all Psalms.

- Warm ivory paper, muted sage and forest greens, restrained gold light, atmospheric distance, translucent washes, finely observed natural forms, and an adult devotional tone.
- One main illustration per chapter in the complete Psalms collection. Related chapters may share an appropriate painting. Individual chapter introductions remain distinct.
- Choose imagery from the chapter or an explicitly symbolic response to its themes. Allow sorrow, darkness, uncertainty, and judgment their place rather than making every scene cheerful.
- No geometric placeholders, progress trail, completion game, text inside the artwork, or purported portrait of God. Keep Scripture, artwork, explanation, and personal reflection distinct.
- Use the selected Psalm 23:2 image as a visual reference for new art. Keep a wide 3:2 composition with important subjects inside the central 80%. Show the full painting on chapter pages; avoid cutting off the focal subject.
- Original Psalm 23 and Psalm 46 detailed studies remain available separately. Their section paintings follow the same standard.

## Artwork collection and provenance

The 24-scene collection comprises six Psalm 23 paintings, six Psalm 46 paintings, and twelve additional thematic paintings. Images were created with the built-in image generation tool. The file `generation-prompts.json` records the Psalm 46 and thematic prompts; the Psalm 23 prompt brief is in `../psalm23/README.md`.

Production assets are 1280 × 853 WebP files. All are included in the Android bundle and offline cache. They are symbolic illustrations, not historical reconstructions, documentary images, or copies of third-party commentary.

## Editorial standard

All 150 chapters retain the exact BSB verse rows, including headings carried in the source. Each has its own concise chapter introduction; literary context and reading prompts are labeled editorial. Do not manufacture a historical setting, assume every psalm is by David, turn judgment language into a call to violence, or treat poetic trust as a guarantee against harm. Link characters when their names are actually in the chapter or heading. Reflection remains optional.

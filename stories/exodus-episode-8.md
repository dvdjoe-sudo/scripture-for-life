Exodus Episode 8: The Dwelling Is Built
Exodus 35 to 40, Berean Standard Bible

After the calf and the renewed covenant, Moses came back down with a radiant face and set the people to work.

Moses assembled the whole Israelite community and told them what the LORD had commanded them to do. For six days work was to be done, but the seventh day would be holy to them, a Sabbath of complete rest to the LORD. Whoever did any work on it was to be put to death. They were not even to kindle a fire in any of their dwellings on the Sabbath day.

Then Moses told all the Israelites to take an offering for the LORD from what they had. Everyone whose heart was willing was to bring it: gold, silver, and bronze; blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen; goat hair; ram skins dyed red and other durable leather; acacia wood; olive oil for the light; spices for the anointing oil and for the fragrant incense; onyx stones and other gems to be mounted on the ephod and breastpiece. All the skilled among them were to come and make everything the LORD had commanded.

The people heard what the gold and cloth and wood would become. They heard that Bezalel and Oholiab had been filled with God's Spirit for the work, and that God had given them and the skilled workers the ability to teach others. "So all the men and women of the Israelites whose hearts prompted them brought a freewill offering to the LORD for all the work that the LORD through Moses had commanded them to do."

The response was so great that it became a problem. The people kept bringing freewill offerings morning after morning. The skilled workers who were doing the work on the sanctuary came and told Moses that the people were bringing more than enough for doing the work the LORD had commanded to be done. "After Moses had given an order, they sent a proclamation throughout the camp: 'No man or woman should make anything else as an offering for the sanctuary.' So the people were restrained from bringing more, since what they already had was more than enough to perform all the work."

This time Moses was not only giving God's plans. He was watching Israel build them.

Bezalel made the ark of acacia wood, overlaid it with pure gold inside and out, made the gold molding, cast four gold rings, and made poles of acacia wood overlaid with gold for carrying it. He made the mercy seat of pure gold, with two cherubim of hammered gold at its ends. He made the table and overlaid it with pure gold. He made the lampstand of pure gold, hammered out, with its cups, buds, and blossoms, six branches, and seven lamps. He made the altar of incense and the anointing oil and the fragrant incense.

The tabernacle rose piece by piece. They made ten curtains of finely twisted linen and blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, with cherubim worked into them. They made curtains of goat hair for the tent over the tabernacle. They made upright frames of acacia wood standing on silver bases, with gold crossbars. They made the veil of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and finely twisted linen, with cherubim worked into it, hung on four posts overlaid with gold. They made the curtain for the entrance to the tent and hung it on five posts with gold hooks and bronze bases.

They made the bronze altar, with its horns, pots, shovels, basins, forks, and firepans, all of bronze. They made the bronze basin and its bronze stand. They made the courtyard with its linen curtains, posts, silver hooks and bands, and bronze bases, and its entrance curtain in the embroidered blue, purple, and scarlet pattern.

Then the sacred garments came together. They made the ephod of gold and blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and finely twisted linen. They mounted the onyx stones in gold filigree settings and engraved them with the names of the sons of Israel, six on one and six on the other, in order of birth. They made the breastpiece, doubled and square, in the same colors and workmanship, with four rows of precious stones, twelve in all, each engraved like a seal with the name of one of the twelve tribes. They made the robe of the ephod entirely of blue cloth, with pomegranates and gold bells around its hem. They made tunics, sashes, and headbands of fine linen for Aaron and his sons. They made the turban of fine linen and the plate of pure gold engraved like a seal: HOLY TO THE LORD.

When the work on the tabernacle was completed, the Israelites had done everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses. Then they brought the tabernacle to Moses: the tent and all its furnishings, its clasps, frames, crossbars, posts and bases; the covering of ram skins dyed red and the covering of durable leather and the shielding curtain; the ark of the testimony with its poles and the mercy seat; the table with all its articles and the bread of the Presence; the pure gold lampstand with its row of lamps and all its accessories, and the oil for the light; the gold altar, the anointing oil, the fragrant incense, and the curtain for the entrance to the tent; the bronze altar with its bronze grating, its poles and all its utensils; the basin with its stand; the curtains of the courtyard with its posts and bases, and the curtain for the entrance to the courtyard; the ropes and tent pegs for the courtyard and all the furnishings for the service of the tabernacle, the tent of meeting; and the woven garments for ministering in the sanctuary, both the sacred garments for Aaron the priest and the garments for his sons when serving as priests.

"And Moses inspected all the work and saw that they had accomplished it just as the LORD had commanded. So Moses blessed them."

Then the LORD told Moses to set up the tabernacle, the tent of meeting, on the first day of the first month. Moses was to place the ark of the testimony in it and shield it with the veil. He was to bring in the table and set out what belonged on it. He was to bring in the lampstand and set up its lamps. He was to place the gold altar of incense in front of the ark of the testimony and put the curtain at the entrance to the tabernacle. He was to set the altar of burnt offering in front of the entrance to the tabernacle, the tent of meeting. He was to place the basin between the tent of meeting and the altar and put water in it. He was to set up the courtyard around it and put the curtain at the entrance to the courtyard.

Then he was to take the anointing oil and anoint the tabernacle and everything in it. He was to consecrate it and all its furnishings, and it would be holy. He was to anoint the altar of burnt offering and all its utensils and consecrate the altar, and it would be most holy. He was to anoint the basin and its stand and consecrate them. Then he was to bring Aaron and his sons to the entrance to the tent of meeting and wash them with water. He was to put the sacred garments on Aaron, anoint him, and consecrate him so he could serve as priest. He was to bring Aaron's sons, clothe them with tunics, and anoint them just as he anointed their father, so they could serve as priests. Their anointing would be to a priesthood that would continue throughout their generations.

"So the tabernacle was set up on the first day of the first month of the second year." Moses set it up and put its bases in place, erected its frames, inserted its crossbars, and set up its posts. He spread the tent over the tabernacle and put the covering over the tent, as the LORD had commanded. He took the testimony and placed it in the ark, attached the poles to the ark, and placed the mercy seat on top of the ark. Then he brought the ark into the tabernacle and hung the shielding curtain and shielded the ark of the testimony, as the LORD had commanded.

He placed the table in the tent of meeting on the north side of the tabernacle outside the veil and set out the bread on it before the LORD. He placed the lampstand in the tent of meeting opposite the table on the south side of the tabernacle and set up the lamps before the LORD. He placed the gold altar in the tent of meeting in front of the veil and burned fragrant incense on it. He put the curtain at the entrance to the tabernacle. He set the altar of burnt offering near the entrance to the tabernacle, the tent of meeting, and offered on it burnt offerings and grain offerings. He placed the basin between the tent of meeting and the altar and put water in it for washing. Moses, Aaron, and his sons used it to wash their hands and feet. They washed whenever they entered the tent of meeting or approached the altar, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

Moses set up the courtyard around the tabernacle and altar, and put up the curtain at the entrance to the courtyard. In this way Moses finished the work.

"Then the cloud covered the Tent of Meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle. Moses was unable to enter the Tent of Meeting because the cloud had settled on it, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."

The God who had made the sanctuary's pattern on the mountain now made it His dwelling. Israel would not move on guesswork. "Whenever the cloud was lifted from above the tabernacle, the Israelites would set out through all the stages of their journey." If the cloud was not lifted, they did not set out until the day it was lifted. "For the cloud of the LORD was over the tabernacle by day, and fire was in the cloud by night, in the sight of all the house of Israel through all their journeys."

The book that opened on slaves making bricks closes on craftsmen finishing a sanctuary. The LORD who saw their affliction, heard their cry, and came down to rescue them now dwelt among them.

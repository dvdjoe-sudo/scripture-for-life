Exodus Episode 7: The Golden Calf
Exodus 32 to 34, Berean Standard Bible

When the people saw that Moses was so long in coming down from the mountain, they gathered around Aaron.

They told him to make them gods who would go before them. As for this fellow Moses who had brought them up out of Egypt, they did not know what had happened to him.

Aaron told them to take off the gold earrings their wives, sons, and daughters were wearing and bring them to him. The people took off their gold earrings and brought them to Aaron. "He took the gold from their hands, and with an engraving tool he fashioned it into a molten calf. And they said, 'These, O Israel, are your gods, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!'"

When Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of the calf and announced that tomorrow there would be a festival to the LORD. The next day the people rose early and sacrificed burnt offerings and presented fellowship offerings. Afterward they sat down to eat and drink and got up to play.

On the mountain, the LORD told Moses to go down, because the people he had brought up out of Egypt had become corrupt. They had been quick to turn away from what God commanded them. They had made themselves an idol cast in the shape of a calf. They had bowed down to it, sacrificed to it, and credited it with bringing them up out of Egypt.

God called them a stiff-necked people. His anger burned. He told Moses to leave Him alone, so that His anger might burn against them and consume them, and He would make Moses into a great nation.

Moses sought the favor of the LORD his God. He asked why God's anger should burn against His own people, whom He had brought out of Egypt with great power and a mighty hand. Why should the Egyptians be able to say that God brought Israel out with evil intent to kill them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the earth? Moses pleaded with God to turn from His fierce anger and relent. He asked Him not to bring disaster on His people. He called on God to remember His servants Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, to whom God had sworn by Himself that He would make their descendants as numerous as the stars in the sky and give them all the land He had promised as their inheritance forever.

Then the LORD relented and did not bring on His people the disaster He had threatened.

Moses turned and went down the mountain with the two tablets of the testimony in his hands. They were inscribed on both sides, front and back. The tablets were the work of God. The writing was the writing of God, engraved on the tablets.

As Moses and Joshua came close to the camp, Joshua heard the sound of the people shouting. He told Moses there was the sound of war in the camp. Moses answered that it was not the sound of victory or defeat. It was the sound of singing he heard.

"As Moses approached the camp and saw the calf and the dancing, he burned with anger and threw the tablets out of his hands, shattering them at the base of the mountain."

Then Moses took the calf the people had made and burned it in the fire. He ground it to powder, scattered it on the water, and made the Israelites drink it.

Moses asked Aaron what these people had done to him that he had led them into such a great sin. Aaron told Moses not to be angry. Moses knew how prone the people were to evil. They had demanded gods to go before them because they did not know what had happened to Moses. Aaron said he had thrown the gold into the fire, and out came this calf.

Moses saw that the people were running wild. Aaron had let them get out of control, to the derision of their enemies. Moses stood at the entrance to the camp and said, "Whoever is for the LORD, come to me." All the Levites rallied to him.

Moses told them the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded each man to strap a sword to his side and go back and forth through the camp from one end to the other, each killing his brother and friend and neighbor. The Levites did as Moses commanded, and that day about three thousand of the people died. Then Moses told them they had been set apart to the LORD that day, because they were against their own sons and brothers, and the LORD had blessed them.

The next day Moses said to the people that they had committed a great sin. He would go up to the LORD. Perhaps he could make atonement for their sin.

So Moses went back to the LORD and said that the people had committed a great sin. They had made themselves gods of gold. He asked God to forgive their sin, but if not, to blot him out of the book God had written.

The LORD replied to Moses that whoever had sinned against Him was the one He would blot out of His book. Now Moses was to go and lead the people to the place God had spoken of. God's angel would go before them. However, when the time came for God to punish, He would punish them for their sin.

"And the LORD sent a plague on the people because of what they had done with the calf that Aaron had made."

After that, the LORD told Moses to leave that place and go up to the land He had promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. He would send an angel before Moses and drive out the Canaanites, Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites. The land was flowing with milk and honey. But God said He would not go with Israel, because they were a stiff-necked people, and He might destroy them on the way.

When the people heard these distressing words, they began to mourn. No one put on any ornaments. The LORD had told Moses to tell the Israelites that they were stiff-necked. If God were to go with them even for a moment, He might destroy them. They were to take off their ornaments, and God would decide what to do with them. The Israelites stopped wearing their ornaments from Mount Horeb onward.

Now Moses used to take a tent and pitch it outside the camp, some distance away. He called it the tent of meeting. It was Moses' own tent for meeting with the LORD, pitched there before the sanctuary from God's pattern had been built. Anyone inquiring of the LORD would go to the tent of meeting outside the camp. Whenever Moses went out to the tent, all the people rose and stood at the entrances to their tents and watched Moses until he entered the tent. As Moses entered, the pillar of cloud would come down and stay at the entrance while the LORD spoke with Moses. Whenever the people saw the pillar of cloud standing at the entrance to the tent, they all stood and worshiped, each at the entrance of his tent. The LORD would speak to Moses face to face, as one speaks to a friend. Then Moses would return to the camp, but his young aide Joshua son of Nun did not leave the tent.

Moses said to the LORD that God had told him to lead these people, but had not let him know whom He would send with him. God had said He knew Moses by name and Moses had found favor with Him. If Moses had found favor in God's eyes, he asked God to teach him His ways so he might know Him and continue to find favor with Him. Moses also reminded God that this nation was His people.

The LORD replied, "My Presence will go with you, and I will give you rest."

Then Moses told Him that if His Presence did not go with Israel, God should not send them up from there. How would anyone know that Moses and God's people had found favor with Him if He did not go with them? What else would distinguish Moses and this people from all the other people on the face of the earth?

The LORD said to Moses that He would do the very thing Moses had asked, because Moses had found favor in His sight and God knew him by name.

Then Moses said to show him God's glory.

The LORD replied, "I will cause all My goodness to pass before you, and I will proclaim My name, the LORD, in your presence. I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion." But God said Moses could not see His face, for no one could see Him and live.

God told Moses there was a place beside Him where he would stand on a rock. When God's glory passed by, He would put Moses in a cleft in the rock and cover him with His hand until He had passed by. Then He would remove His hand, and Moses would see His back, but His face must not be seen.

The LORD told Moses to chisel out two stone tablets like the first ones, and He would write on them the words that were on the first tablets, which Moses had broken. Moses was to be ready in the morning and come up Mount Sinai and present himself there on top of the mountain. No one was to go up with him or be seen anywhere on the mountain. No flocks or herds were to graze in front of the mountain.

Moses chiseled out two stone tablets like the first ones and went up Mount Sinai early in the morning, as the LORD had commanded. He carried the two stone tablets in his hands.

Then the LORD came down in the cloud and stood there with Moses and proclaimed His name. "Then the LORD passed in front of Moses and called out: 'The LORD, the LORD God, is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion and faithfulness, maintaining loving devotion to a thousand generations, forgiving iniquity, transgression, and sin. Yet He will by no means leave the guilty unpunished; He will visit the iniquity of the fathers on their children and grandchildren to the third and fourth generations.'"

Moses bowed to the ground at once and worshiped. He asked that if he had found favor in God's eyes, the Lord would go with Israel. Although this was a stiff-necked people, he asked God to forgive their wickedness and their sin, and take them as His inheritance.

Then the LORD said He was making a covenant. Before all Israel He would do wonders such as had not been created in any land or nation. The people among whom Israel lived would see how awesome is the work the LORD would do for them. Israel was to obey what God commanded that day. He would drive out before them the Amorites, Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites.

Israel had to be careful not to make a treaty with those who lived in the land they were entering, or they would become a snare. The Israelites were to break down their altars, smash their sacred stones, and cut down their Asherah poles. They were to worship no other god, for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God. They were not to make treaties with the people of the land and then be invited to sacrifice to their gods and eat their sacrifices. They were not to choose wives from among them for their sons, or those wives would lead their sons to prostitute themselves to their gods. They were not to make any idols.

The festival commands were given again. Israel was to keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread. For seven days they were to eat bread made without yeast, as commanded, at the appointed time in the month of Aviv, because in that month they came out of Egypt. The first offspring of every womb belonged to God, among livestock, cattle and sheep. A firstborn donkey could be redeemed with a lamb, but if it was not redeemed, its neck was to be broken. All Israel's firstborn sons were to be redeemed. No one was to appear before God empty-handed.

Six days Israel could work, but on the seventh they were to rest. Even during the plowing season and harvest they were to rest. They were to celebrate the Festival of Weeks with the firstfruits of the wheat harvest, and the Festival of Ingathering at the turn of the year. Three times a year all their men were to appear before the Sovereign LORD, the God of Israel. God would drive out nations before them and enlarge their territory, and no one would covet their land when they went up three times each year to appear before the LORD their God.

They were not to offer the blood of God's sacrifices with anything containing yeast, and the sacrifice of the Passover Festival was not to remain until morning. The best of the firstfruits of their soil were to be brought to the house of the LORD their God. They were not to cook a young goat in its mother's milk.

The LORD told Moses to write down these words, for in accordance with them God had made a covenant with Moses and with Israel.

"So Moses was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights without eating bread or drinking water. He wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the Ten Commandments."

When Moses came down from Mount Sinai with the two tablets of the testimony in his hands, he did not know that his face had become radiant because he had spoken with the LORD. When Aaron and all the Israelites saw Moses, the skin of his face was shining, and they were afraid to come near him. But Moses called to them, and Aaron and all the leaders of the community came back to him, and Moses spoke to them. Afterward all the Israelites came near, and he gave them all the commands the LORD had given him on Mount Sinai. When Moses finished speaking to them, he put a veil over his face. Whenever Moses went in before the LORD to speak with Him, he removed the veil until he came out. When he came out and told the Israelites what he had been commanded, they saw that his face was radiant. Then Moses would put the veil back over his face until he went in again to speak with the LORD.

The calf was gone. The tablets were whole again. The covenant had been spoken a second time. And the man who carried God's words back into camp now carried the mark of the mountain on his face.

Genesis Episode 7: A Wife for Isaac and Two Twin Brothers
Genesis 23 to 27, Berean Standard Bible

Sarah died in Hebron, and Abraham's first piece of the promised land was a grave.

Sarah lived 127 years. She died at Kiriath-arba, which is Hebron, in the land of Canaan. Abraham went to mourn for her and to weep over her. Then he rose from beside his dead wife and spoke to the Hittites who lived there. He was a foreigner and stranger among them, and he asked for a burial site so he could bury his dead.

The Hittites called Abraham a mighty prince among them and offered him the finest of their tombs. Abraham bowed and asked them to speak to Ephron son of Zohar for the cave of Machpelah at the end of Ephron's field. Abraham did not want the cave as a favor. He wanted to buy the field for the full price and hold it as his own burial property.

Ephron answered in the hearing of the people and named the whole field, the cave, and the trees within its borders. Abraham weighed out the price in the hearing of the Hittites: 400 shekels of silver, according to the standard of the merchants. The field passed to Abraham as his property in the sight of all who entered the gate of the city. Abraham buried Sarah in the cave of the field of Machpelah near Mamre, at Hebron in the land of Canaan.

The promise had named a whole land. Abraham now owned a field, a cave, and the place where Sarah lay.

By then Abraham was old and well advanced in years, and the LORD had blessed him in every way. He called the oldest servant in his household, the man in charge of all he had, and made him swear by the LORD, the God of heaven and earth. The servant was not to take a wife for Isaac from the daughters of the Canaanites among whom Abraham lived. He was to go to Abraham's country and kindred and get a wife for Isaac there.

The servant asked the hard question. What if the woman was unwilling to come back with him? Should he take Isaac to the land Abraham had left? Abraham said no. Isaac must not go back there. The God who had taken Abraham from his father's house would send His angel before the servant. If the woman was unwilling, the servant would be released from the oath.

The servant took ten of Abraham's camels and departed with gifts for Aram Naharaim, to the city of Nahor. He made the camels kneel by the well outside the city toward evening, when women went out to draw water. There he prayed for a sign specific enough to trust. The woman who offered him a drink and also offered to draw water for his camels would be the one the LORD had appointed for Isaac.

Before he had finished praying, Rebekah came out with her jar on her shoulder. She was the daughter of Bethuel, the son of Milcah and Nahor, Abraham's brother. She filled her jar, offered the servant a drink, and then drew water for all his camels until they had finished drinking. The servant watched her in silence, waiting to learn whether the LORD had made his journey successful.

When the camels had finished, he gave her a gold ring and two gold bracelets and asked whose daughter she was. He asked whether there was room in her father's house for him to spend the night. Rebekah answered and ran to tell her mother's household. Her brother Laban saw the jewelry and heard her words. He came out to the servant by the spring and brought him into the house.

Food was set before the servant, but he would not eat until he had told his errand. He named Abraham, named Isaac, and told how he had prayed at the well before Rebekah came. Bethuel and Laban answered that the matter had come from the LORD and could not be spoken against. Rebekah stood before the servant. He could take her and go, so she could become the wife of Abraham's son.

They called Rebekah and asked her, "Will you go with this man?" "I will go," she replied.

Her family blessed her and sent her away with the servant. In the Negev, Isaac had come from Beer Lahai Roi and was living in the land of the south. Toward evening he went out to the field to meditate and lifted his eyes. Camels were coming. Rebekah lifted her eyes too, saw Isaac, and slipped down from the camel. When she learned who he was, she took her veil and covered herself.

The servant told Isaac everything he had done. "And Isaac brought her into the tent of his mother Sarah and took Rebekah as his wife. And Isaac loved her and was comforted after his mother's death."

Abraham took another wife, Keturah, and she bore him sons. Abraham gave gifts to the sons of his concubines and sent them eastward, away from Isaac, while he was still living. He gave everything he owned to Isaac. Abraham lived 175 years. He breathed his last and died at a good old age, an old man and full of years, and he was gathered to his people. Isaac and Ishmael buried him with Sarah in the cave of Machpelah.

Ishmael's line is named, twelve princes according to their tribes, and Ishmael died at 137.

Isaac's own family began with a familiar grief. Isaac prayed to the LORD on behalf of Rebekah because she was barren. The LORD answered, and Rebekah conceived. The children struggled together inside her. She asked why this was happening to her and went to inquire of the LORD.

The LORD declared to her: "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples from within you will be separated; one people will be stronger than the other, and the older will serve the younger."

When the days to give birth came, there were twins in her womb. The first came out red, covered with hair like a garment, and they named him Esau. Afterward his brother came out with his hand gripping Esau's heel, so he was named Jacob. Isaac was sixty years old when the twins were born.

The boys grew up with different shapes to their lives. Esau became a skillful hunter, a man of the open country. Jacob was a quiet man, staying among the tents. Isaac loved Esau because he ate of his game. Rebekah loved Jacob.

Once Jacob was cooking stew when Esau came in from the field famished. Esau asked for some of the red stew because he was exhausted. Jacob answered that Esau must first sell him his birthright. Esau said he was about to die, and asked what good the birthright was to him. Jacob made him swear. Esau swore and sold his birthright for bread and lentil stew. He ate, drank, rose, and went away. Genesis says, "Thus Esau despised his birthright."

A famine came in the days of Isaac, separate from the famine in Abraham's days. Isaac went to Abimelech, king of the Philistines, in Gerar. The LORD appeared to Isaac and told him not to go down to Egypt. He was to live in the land God told him about. God repeated the promise of land, offspring, and blessing to the nations. Isaac stayed in Gerar.

In Gerar, Isaac used Abraham's old fear. When the men of the place asked about Rebekah, he said she was his sister because he feared he might die on account of her. After Isaac had been there a long time, Abimelech looked down from a window and saw Isaac caressing Rebekah. The king called Isaac and confronted him. A man of the people might have slept with Rebekah and brought guilt on them. Isaac admitted he had feared for his life. Abimelech ordered all the people not to touch Isaac or his wife, on pain of death.

Isaac planted crops in that land and reaped a hundredfold in the same year because the LORD blessed him. He became rich and kept gaining wealth until he was very wealthy. He had flocks, herds, and many servants, and the Philistines envied him. They filled with earth the wells Abraham's servants had dug. Abimelech told Isaac to move away because he had become too powerful.

Isaac dug again in the valley. His herdsmen quarreled with Gerar's herdsmen over water. One well was named Esek, because they disputed it. Another was named Sitnah, because they accused him. Then Isaac moved and dug another well. No one quarreled over it, so he named it Rehoboth, because the LORD had made room for them to flourish in the land.

From there Isaac went up to Beersheba. That night the LORD appeared to him and repeated the promise as the God of Abraham. Isaac built an altar there and called on the name of the LORD. His servants dug a well. Abimelech came from Gerar with Ahuzzath his adviser and Phicol the commander of his forces. When Isaac asked why they had come after hating him and sending him away, they replied, "We can plainly see that the LORD has been with you." They made a covenant and feasted. Isaac sent them away in peace. That same day Isaac's servants told him about water they had found.

When Esau was forty years old, he married Judith daughter of Beeri the Hittite and Basemath daughter of Elon the Hittite. They made life bitter for Isaac and Rebekah.

Isaac was old, and his eyes were so dim he could not see. He called Esau and told him to take his weapons, hunt game, prepare the tasty food Isaac loved, and bring it to him so Isaac could bless Esau before he died. Rebekah was listening. When Esau went to the field, Rebekah spoke to Jacob. She had heard Isaac's words, and she had a plan to place Jacob in front of them. Jacob was to bring two young goats. Rebekah would prepare the food Isaac loved. Jacob would carry it to his father, who might bless Jacob before his death.

Jacob raised the obvious problem. Esau was hairy, and he was smooth. If Isaac touched him, Jacob would seem like a trickster and bring a curse instead of a blessing. Rebekah answered that the curse would fall on her. She told Jacob to obey her voice.

Jacob brought the goats. Rebekah cooked the food, put Esau's best clothes on Jacob, and covered his hands and the smooth part of his neck with goat skins. Then she placed the food and bread in Jacob's hands.

Jacob went to his father and said he was Esau, his firstborn. Isaac asked how the game had been found so quickly. Jacob said the LORD his God had granted him success. Isaac told him to come near so he could feel whether Jacob was really Esau. The voice was Jacob's voice, but the hands were the hands of Esau. Isaac ate, drank wine, drew Jacob near, and kissed him. He smelled the clothing, the smell of a field the LORD had blessed, and spoke the blessing.

"May peoples serve you and nations bow down to you. May you be the master of your brothers, and may the sons of your mother bow down to you. May those who curse you be cursed, and those who bless you be blessed."

Jacob had scarcely left when Esau came in from hunting. He prepared food and brought it to his father. Isaac trembled violently when he learned who had come before him. The blessing spoken over Jacob would stand. Esau cried out with an exceedingly great and bitter cry and begged his father to bless him too. Isaac answered that Jacob had come with deceit and taken Esau's blessing.

Esau still received words from his father about living by the sword and serving his brother, and about a time when he would grow restless and break Jacob's yoke from his neck. The words did not cool his anger. Esau hated Jacob and planned to kill him after the days of mourning for Isaac.

Rebekah heard Esau's plan. She sent Jacob away to Laban, her brother in Haran, until Esau's fury turned aside. To Isaac, she spoke of her weariness with the Hittite women Esau had married. If Jacob married one of them, she said her life would be worth nothing. Isaac called Jacob, blessed him, charged him not to take a wife from the Canaanite women, and sent him to Paddan-aram to take a wife from the daughters of Laban.

Jacob obeyed his father and mother. He left Beersheba and went toward Haran, carrying a stolen blessing and a father's command.

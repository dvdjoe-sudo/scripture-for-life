Genesis Episode 10: Joseph in Egypt
Genesis 39 to 41, Berean Standard Bible

Joseph arrived in Egypt as property, and the house he served began to prosper around him.

The LORD was with Joseph in the house of Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and the captain of the guard. "And the LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man, serving in the household of his Egyptian master." Potiphar saw that the LORD was with Joseph and that the LORD made everything Joseph did succeed. Joseph found favor in Potiphar's eyes and became his personal attendant. Potiphar put Joseph in charge of his household and entrusted everything he owned to Joseph's care. From that time, the LORD blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake, in the house and in the field. Potiphar left everything he had in Joseph's care and concerned himself only with the food he ate.

Joseph was handsome in form and appearance. After some time, Potiphar's wife cast her eyes on Joseph and told him to lie with her. Joseph refused. He told her Potiphar had withheld nothing from him except her, because she was Potiphar's wife. Joseph said, "So how could I do such a great evil and sin against God?" She spoke to Joseph day after day, but he refused to lie with her or even be with her.

One day Joseph went into the house to do his work, and none of the household servants was inside. Potiphar's wife caught him by his garment and demanded that he lie with her. Joseph left his garment in her hand, ran outside, and fled. She kept the garment and reshaped the event into an accusation. She told the servants that the Hebrew slave had come to insult them and had fled when she screamed. When Potiphar came home, she repeated the story. His anger burned, and he put Joseph into the prison where the king's prisoners were confined.

Joseph's obedience had landed him in a cell. Genesis does not smooth that fact. It says the LORD was with Joseph there too. The LORD showed him kindness and gave him favor in the eyes of the prison warden. The warden put Joseph in charge of all the prisoners who were in the prison, and Joseph became responsible for everything done there. The warden did not concern himself with anything under Joseph's care, because the LORD was with Joseph and gave him success in whatever he did.

After some time, Pharaoh's chief cupbearer and chief baker offended their master. Pharaoh put them in custody in the house of the captain of the guard, in the same prison where Joseph was confined. The captain put Joseph in charge of them, and they remained in custody for some time.

One night both men had dreams, each with its own meaning. In the morning Joseph saw they were dejected and asked why their faces were so sad. They told him they had dreamed dreams and no one could interpret them. Joseph answered that interpretations belong to God and asked them to tell him the dreams.

The chief cupbearer told his dream first. A vine stood before him with three branches. It budded, blossomed, and produced clusters of ripe grapes. Pharaoh's cup was in his hand, and he took the grapes, squeezed them into Pharaoh's cup, and placed the cup in Pharaoh's hand. Joseph said the three branches were three days. Within three days Pharaoh would lift up the cupbearer's head and restore him to his position. He would place Pharaoh's cup in his hand as before. Joseph asked him to remember Joseph when it went well with him, show him kindness, mention him to Pharaoh, and get him out of that house. Joseph had been taken forcibly from the land of the Hebrews, and in Egypt he had done nothing to deserve the pit.

Seeing that the interpretation was favorable, the baker told his dream. Three baskets of bread were on his head. In the top basket were baked goods for Pharaoh, and birds were eating them out of the basket on his head. Joseph said the three baskets were three days. Within three days Pharaoh would lift the baker's head off and hang him on a tree, and the birds would eat his flesh.

On the third day, Pharaoh's birthday, Pharaoh made a feast for all his servants. He lifted up the head of the chief cupbearer and restored him to his position, so the cupbearer placed the cup in Pharaoh's hand. He hanged the chief baker, just as Joseph had interpreted to them.

"The chief cupbearer, however, did not remember Joseph; he forgot all about him."

Two full years passed.

Pharaoh had a dream and stood by the Nile. Seven cows came up from the river, sleek and fat, and grazed among the reeds. After them seven other cows came up, ugly and thin. The thin cows stood beside the fat cows on the bank and ate them. Pharaoh awoke. He fell asleep again and dreamed of grain. Seven heads of grain, full and good, grew on one stalk. After them seven other heads sprouted, thin and scorched by the east wind. The thin heads swallowed up the full heads. Pharaoh awoke and realized it was a dream. In the morning his mind was troubled. He sent for the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men and told them his dreams, but no one could interpret them.

Then the chief cupbearer finally remembered. He told Pharaoh about his offense, the baker, the prison, and the young Hebrew servant of the captain of the guard who had interpreted their dreams exactly as events happened. Pharaoh sent for Joseph. Joseph was brought quickly out of the dungeon. He shaved, changed his clothes, and came before Pharaoh.

Pharaoh told Joseph he had heard Joseph could interpret a dream. Joseph answered, "I myself cannot do it," and added, "but God will give Pharaoh a sound answer."

Pharaoh told the dreams again. Joseph said the two dreams were one. God had revealed to Pharaoh what He was about to do. The seven good cows and seven good heads were seven years of great abundance across Egypt. The seven thin cows and seven thin heads were seven years of famine that would follow. The famine would be so severe that the abundance would be forgotten in the land. The dream had come in two forms because the matter had been firmly decided by God, and God would do it soon.

Joseph then gave Pharaoh a plan. Pharaoh should look for a discerning and wise man and put him in charge of the land of Egypt. Overseers should collect a fifth of the produce during the seven years of abundance. All the food of those good years should be gathered and stored under Pharaoh's authority in the cities. That food would be a reserve for the land during the seven years of famine, so the land would survive.

The proposal pleased Pharaoh and all his officials. Pharaoh asked them, "Can we find anyone like this man, in whom the Spirit of God abides?" He told Joseph that since God had made all this known to him, no one was as discerning and wise as Joseph. Joseph would be over Pharaoh's house, and all Pharaoh's people would submit to Joseph's word. Only in the throne would Pharaoh be greater.

Pharaoh put his signet ring on Joseph's hand, dressed him in fine linen, and put a gold chain around his neck. Joseph rode in Pharaoh's second chariot, with men calling out before him, "Bow the knee!" Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-paneah and gave him Asenath, daughter of Potiphera priest of On, as his wife.

Joseph was thirty years old when he entered the service of Pharaoh, king of Egypt. He went out from Pharaoh's presence and traveled throughout Egypt.

During the seven years of abundance, the land produced in great plenty. Joseph gathered all the food and stored it in the cities, putting the food of the fields around each city in that city. Joseph stored up so much grain, like the sand of the sea, that he stopped keeping record because it was beyond measure.

Before the years of famine came, two sons were born to Joseph by Asenath. Joseph named the firstborn Manasseh, saying God had made him forget all his trouble and all his father's house. He named the second Ephraim, saying God had made him fruitful in the land of his affliction.

The seven years of abundance in Egypt came to an end. The seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. There was famine in all the lands, but in Egypt there was bread. When all the land of Egypt became hungry, the people cried to Pharaoh for bread. Pharaoh told them to go to Joseph and do whatever he said.

The famine was spread over all the face of the earth. Joseph opened all the storehouses and sold grain to the Egyptians, for the famine was severe in the land of Egypt. All the earth came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, because the famine was severe in all the earth.

Among the lands touched by famine was Canaan, where Joseph's father still lived.

Genesis Episode 12: Goshen, Blessings, and Burial
Genesis 46 to 50, Berean Standard Bible

Israel set out with everything he had, and at Beersheba the old promise met him before Egypt did.

Jacob offered sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac. God spoke to Israel in visions of the night and called his name twice. Jacob answered that he was there. God said He was the God of Jacob's father. Then God told him, "Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for I will make you into a great nation there. I will go down with you to Egypt, and I will surely bring you back. And Joseph's own hands will close your eyes."

Jacob left Beersheba. His sons carried him, their wives, and their little ones in the wagons Pharaoh had sent. They took their livestock and the goods they had acquired in Canaan, and Jacob came into Egypt with all his offspring. Genesis names the sons, grandsons, daughters, and granddaughters who made the journey. The text counts seventy persons of the house of Jacob who came into Egypt.

Joseph prepared his chariot and went up to meet Israel his father in Goshen. He presented himself to Jacob, fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a long time. Israel told Joseph he was ready to die, since he had seen Joseph's face and knew Joseph was still alive.

Joseph told his brothers and his father's household that he would go up and report to Pharaoh. They had come from Canaan, and they were shepherds who had kept livestock from their youth. When Pharaoh called them and asked their occupation, they were to say exactly that. Joseph had a reason for the plain answer. Every shepherd was detestable to the Egyptians, so Pharaoh would let Jacob's household settle in the land of Goshen.

Joseph chose five brothers and presented them to Pharaoh. Pharaoh asked their occupation. The brothers answered that they were shepherds, as their fathers had been. They had come to live in Egypt for a while because the famine was heavy in Canaan and there was no pasture for their flocks. They asked to live in Goshen. Pharaoh spoke to Joseph. The land of Egypt lay before him. Joseph was to settle his father and brothers in the best of the land, in Goshen. If Joseph knew capable men among them, Pharaoh wanted them put in charge of Pharaoh's livestock.

Joseph brought in Jacob and presented him before Pharaoh. Jacob blessed Pharaoh. Pharaoh asked Jacob how many years he had lived. Jacob replied, "My travels have lasted 130 years." Then he added, "My years have been few and hard, and they have not matched the years of the travels of my fathers." Then Jacob blessed Pharaoh and went out from his presence.

Joseph settled his father and brothers and gave them a possession in the best of the land, in the district of Rameses, as Pharaoh had instructed. Joseph provided food for his father, his brothers, and all his father's household according to the number of their children.

Now the famine tightened on people who did not have Joseph's storehouses in the family. There was no bread in all the land, for the famine was very severe. The land of Egypt and the land of Canaan languished because of the famine. Joseph gathered all the money found in Egypt and Canaan in exchange for the grain people bought, and he brought the money into Pharaoh's house.

When the money from Egypt and Canaan was gone, all the Egyptians came to Joseph and asked for bread. They did not want to die in his presence because their money had run out. Joseph told them to bring their livestock, and he gave them food in exchange for horses, flocks, herds, and donkeys. He supplied them with food that year in exchange for all their livestock.

When that year ended, the people came again. They would not hide from Joseph that their money was gone and their herds had been given to him. Nothing was left before him except their bodies and their land. They asked Joseph to buy them and their land for bread. They would be servants to Pharaoh, and Pharaoh would give them seed so they might live, not die, and so the land would not become desolate.

Joseph bought all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh. Every Egyptian sold his field because the famine was severe on them. Joseph reduced the people to servitude from one end of Egypt's border to the other. Only the land of the priests he did not buy, because the priests had a fixed allowance from Pharaoh and ate from that allowance. Joseph told the people he had bought them and their land for Pharaoh. He gave them seed for planting. At harvest, a fifth belonged to Pharaoh. Four fifths were theirs for seed, food, and their households. The people said Joseph had saved their lives. They asked to find favor in his eyes and said they would be Pharaoh's servants. Joseph made it a statute concerning the land of Egypt, still in place when Genesis was written: a fifth belongs to Pharaoh. Only the priests' land did not become Pharaoh's.

Israel settled in Egypt, in the land of Goshen. His household acquired property there. They were fruitful and multiplied greatly. Jacob lived in Egypt seventeen years, so the years of Jacob's life were 147.

When the time drew near for Israel to die, he called Joseph and made him swear an oath. Joseph was not to bury him in Egypt. When Jacob slept with his fathers, Joseph was to carry him out of Egypt and bury him in their burying place. Joseph said he would do as Jacob had spoken. Jacob told him to swear, and Joseph swore. Then Israel bowed himself on the head of the bed.

After this, Joseph was told that his father was ill. He took his two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim, and went to Jacob. Israel strengthened himself and sat up in bed. He told Joseph that God Almighty had appeared to him at Luz in the land of Canaan and blessed him there. God had promised to make him fruitful, multiply him, make him into a company of peoples, and give the land to his offspring after him as an everlasting possession.

Jacob adopted Joseph's sons. Ephraim and Manasseh would be Jacob's, just as Reuben and Simeon were his. Children born to Joseph after them would be Joseph's and would be called by the names of their brothers in their inheritance. Jacob remembered Rachel, who had died in Canaan on the way when there was still distance to go to Ephrath. He had buried her there on the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem.

Israel's eyes were heavy because of old age, and he could not see. When he saw Joseph's sons, he asked who they were. Joseph said they were the sons God had given him in Egypt. Jacob told Joseph to bring them near so he could bless them. Joseph brought them near, and Jacob kissed them and embraced them. Israel told Joseph he had not expected to see Joseph's face, and God had let him see his offspring too.

Joseph took Ephraim with his right hand toward Israel's left hand, and Manasseh with his left hand toward Israel's right hand, because Manasseh was the firstborn. He brought the boys near. "But Israel stretched out his right hand and put it on the head of Ephraim, the younger; and crossing his hands, he put his left on Manasseh's head, although Manasseh was the firstborn."

Israel blessed Joseph through the boys and spoke of the God before whom Abraham and Isaac had walked, the God who had been his shepherd all his life to that day, and the angel who had redeemed him from all evil. He asked that the boys be blessed, that his name and the names of Abraham and Isaac live on in them, and that they grow into a multitude in the midst of the earth.

When Joseph saw that his father had laid his right hand on Ephraim's head, it displeased him. He took hold of his father's hand to move it from Ephraim's head to Manasseh's head. Joseph told him Manasseh was the firstborn and should receive the right hand. His father refused. Jacob said he knew. Manasseh would also become a people and would be great, but his younger brother would be greater than he. Ephraim's offspring would become a multitude of nations. Israel blessed them that day and set Ephraim before Manasseh.

Israel told Joseph he was about to die, but God would be with Joseph and his brothers and would bring them back to the land of their fathers. Jacob also gave Joseph one portion more than his brothers, a portion Jacob said he had taken from the hand of the Amorites with his sword and bow.

Then Jacob called his sons to gather around so he could tell them what would happen to them in days to come. The blessing at the bedside became twelve different futures.

Reuben, Jacob's firstborn and the beginning of his strength, heard that he would no longer have the preeminence. He had been turbulent as the waters, and he had gone up to his father's bed and defiled it. Simeon and Levi were paired as brothers whose swords were weapons of violence. Jacob cursed their anger, fierce and cruel, and said they would be divided in Jacob and scattered in Israel. They would not share a single inheritance in one place.

Judah stood taller in his father's words. His brothers would praise him. His hand would be on the neck of his enemies, and his father's sons would bow down to him. Judah was a lion's cub and a lion who stooped down. No one would dare rouse him. Jacob said, "The scepter will not depart from Judah, nor the staff from between his feet, until Shiloh comes and the allegiance of the nations is his." Judah's foal would be tied to the vine, and his garments washed in wine and the blood of grapes.

Zebulun would dwell at the shore of the sea and be a harbor for ships, with his border beside Sidon. Issachar was a strong donkey lying between sheep pens. He saw rest was good and the land pleasant, so he bent his shoulder to bear a burden and became a servant at forced labor. Dan would judge his people as one of Israel's tribes, a serpent by the road and a viper by the path that bites the horse's heels so its rider falls backward. In Dan's line Jacob paused and said, "I await Your salvation, O LORD."

Gad would be raided by a raiding party, but he would raid at their heels. Asher's food would be rich, and he would yield royal delicacies. Naphtali was a doe set free that gives beautiful words.

Joseph received the longest blessing after Judah. He was a fruitful bough by a spring, with branches running over the wall. Archers bitterly attacked him, shot at him, and harassed him. But his bow remained steady, and his arms were strengthened by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob, by the Shepherd, the Rock of Israel. Jacob spoke of the God of his father who helped him, the Almighty who blessed him with blessings of heaven above and the deep that crouches beneath, blessings of the breasts and of the womb. The blessings on Joseph surpassed the blessings of Jacob's parents, reaching to the boundaries of the everlasting hills. They rested on Joseph's head, on the prince among his brothers.

Benjamin was a ravenous wolf. In the morning he devoured the prey. At evening he divided the spoil.

All these were the twelve tribes of Israel, and this was what Jacob spoke to them when he blessed each with the blessing appropriate to him. Then Jacob charged them about his burial. He was about to be gathered to his people. They were to bury him with his fathers in the cave in the field of Ephron the Hittite, the cave in the field at Machpelah near Mamre in the land of Canaan, the field Abraham had bought from Ephron as burial property. Abraham and Sarah were buried there. Isaac and Rebekah were buried there. Leah was buried there. When Jacob had finished commanding his sons, he drew his feet into the bed, breathed his last, and was gathered to his people.

Joseph fell on his father's face and wept over him and kissed him. Joseph commanded the physicians in his service to embalm his father. The physicians embalmed Israel, taking forty days, the time required for embalming. The Egyptians wept for Jacob for seventy days.

When the days of mourning had passed, Joseph spoke to Pharaoh's household. He asked them to tell Pharaoh that his father had made him swear to bury him in the tomb Jacob had prepared in Canaan. Joseph asked permission to go up, bury his father, and return. Pharaoh told him to go up and bury his father, as he had sworn.

Joseph went up to bury his father. With him went all the servants of Pharaoh, the elders of Pharaoh's household, and all the elders of the land of Egypt. Jacob's whole household went, and Joseph's brothers, and his father's household. Only their little ones, flocks, and herds were left in the land of Goshen. Chariots and horsemen also went up with him. It was a very great company.

When they came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, they lamented there with a very great and grievous lamentation. Joseph made a mourning for his father seven days. The Canaanites who lived in the land saw the mourning at Atad and said it was a grievous mourning for the Egyptians. The place was called Abel-mizraim, the Mourning of Egypt. Jacob's sons did for him as he had commanded them. They carried him to the land of Canaan and buried him in the cave of the field at Machpelah, near Mamre, the field Abraham had bought from Ephron the Hittite as burial property. After he had buried his father, Joseph returned to Egypt with his brothers and all who had gone up with him.

Once Jacob was buried, Joseph's brothers saw that their father was dead. They said Joseph might bear a grudge and pay them back in full for all the evil they had done to him. They sent a message to Joseph saying their father had commanded before he died that Joseph should forgive the transgression of his brothers and their sin, because they had done evil to him. They asked Joseph to forgive the transgression of the servants of the God of his father. Joseph wept when they spoke to him.

His brothers also came and fell down before him. They said they were his servants. Joseph told them not to fear. He asked whether he was in the place of God. Then Joseph gave Genesis its clearest sentence about the pit, the prison, and the throne.

"As for you, what you intended against me for evil, God intended for good, in order to accomplish a day like this, to preserve the lives of many people."

Joseph told them not to fear. He would provide for them and their little ones. He comforted them and spoke kindly to them.

Joseph remained in Egypt, he and his father's household. Joseph lived 110 years. He saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children of Machir, the son of Manasseh, were also raised on Joseph's knees. Joseph said to his brothers that he was about to die, but God would surely attend to them and bring them up out of that land to the land He had sworn to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Joseph made the sons of Israel take an oath: "God will surely attend to you, and then you must carry my bones up from this place."

So Joseph died at the age of 110 years. They embalmed him, and he was placed in a coffin in Egypt.

Genesis ends in Egypt, with Jacob buried in the promised land, Joseph's coffin still waiting, and God's promise pointed beyond the border.

Genesis Episode 4: Nations and Babel
Genesis 10 to 11, Berean Standard Bible

The ark had emptied into a world that needed people again.

Genesis does not rush past that filling. It names the sons of Noah and follows their families outward: Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and then sons, grandsons, towns, coastlands, and nations. The reader meets the world the way Noah's descendants would have met it, one family at a time, with new names spreading farther from the ark than anyone in Noah's house had ever lived.

Japheth's line moved toward the coastlands. From his sons came peoples divided by land and language, maritime peoples who spread into their territories. Ham's line ran through Cush, Egypt, Put, and Canaan. In that line Genesis pauses on Nimrod, a mighty hunter before the LORD and the first on earth to be a mighty man. His kingdom began with Babel in the land of Shinar, and from that land he went to Assyria and built Nineveh and other great cities. Egypt's line produced peoples whose names later fill Israel's story. Canaan's line reached from Sidon toward Gerar and Gaza, and toward Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboiim. The map is already carrying trouble in some of its names.

Shem's line carried the family line forward. Shem was the father of Eber, and through Eber the line moved toward Peleg. In Peleg's days the earth was divided. Genesis gives the sentence without stopping to explain it, then keeps walking the line down toward Terah. By the end of the list, the whole earth has been populated from Noah's sons, clan by clan, language by language, land by land.

Then the story pulls back to a plain in Shinar.

At that time the whole earth had one language and the same words. As people migrated from the east, they found a plain in the land of Shinar and settled there. They made bricks to use for stone and used tar for mortar. The work gave them an idea larger than shelter.

They said they would build a city for themselves, and a tower with its top in the heavens. They wanted to make a name for themselves. They did not want to be scattered over the face of the whole earth.

The building rose from a settled people who had one speech and one plan. Then the LORD came down to see the city and the tower that the sons of men were building.

The LORD said, "If they have begun to do this as one people speaking the same language, then nothing they devise will be beyond them. Come, let Us go down and confuse their language, so that they will not understand one another's speech."

The work stopped in the middle of human speech. A worker could call for brick, mortar, help, or time to rest, and the man beside him could not understand the words. The LORD scattered them from there over the face of all the earth, and they stopped building the city.

Its name became Babel, because there the LORD confused the language of the whole earth. The scattering the builders feared came from the place where they tried to prevent it.

Genesis has just filled the earth with nations. Now it narrows again.

The line of Shem continues in names and ages. Arphaxad. Shelah. Eber. Peleg. Reu. Serug. Nahor. Terah. Fathers live, sons are born, years stack up, and the wide map begins to tighten around one household. Terah became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Haran became the father of Lot, then Haran died in Ur of the Chaldeans, in the land of his birth, while his father Terah was still living.

Abram married Sarai. Nahor married Milcah, the daughter of Haran. Sarai was barren. She had no child.

Terah took Abram his son, Lot the son of Haran, and Sarai his daughter-in-law, and they set out from Ur of the Chaldeans to go to the land of Canaan. They did not reach Canaan. They came to Haran and settled there.

Terah lived 205 years, and he died in Haran.

So the nations covered the earth, each with its own language, and the line from Shem rested in a household that had stopped short of Canaan. Abram was in Haran. His father was dead. His wife Sarai had no child.

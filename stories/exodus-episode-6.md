Exodus Episode 6: Plans for the Dwelling
Exodus 25 to 31, Berean Standard Bible

Moses was still on the mountain. Under the cloud, Israel had promised to obey. Inside the cloud, God began to speak about a tent.

The LORD told Moses to tell the Israelites to bring Him an offering. From everyone whose heart prompted him to give, Moses was to receive the offering: gold, silver, and bronze; blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen; goat hair; ram skins dyed red and other durable leather; acacia wood; olive oil for the light; spices for the anointing oil and for fragrant incense; onyx stones and other gems to be mounted on the ephod and breastpiece.

God did not only want gifts. He named the purpose. The Israelites were to make a sanctuary for Him, and He would dwell among them. Moses was to make the tabernacle and all its furnishings exactly like the pattern he was shown on the mountain.

First came the ark.

It was to be made of acacia wood, two and a half cubits long, a cubit and a half wide, and a cubit and a half high. It was to be overlaid with pure gold, inside and out, with a gold molding around it. Four gold rings were to be cast for its feet, two on each side. Poles of acacia wood overlaid with gold were to be made and put through the rings so the ark could be carried. The poles were to stay in the rings. They were not to be removed. Into the ark Moses was to put the testimony God would give him.

On top of the ark God set the mercy seat, a cover of pure gold the same length and width as the ark. Two cherubim of hammered gold were to be made, one at each end of the cover, of one piece with it. The cherubim were to spread their wings upward, overshadowing the cover with their wings. They were to face each other, looking toward the mercy seat. God told Moses He would meet with him there, from above the mercy seat, between the cherubim on the ark of the testimony, and speak with him about all He commanded for Israel.

Then came the table for the Bread of the Presence. It too was acacia wood overlaid with pure gold, with a gold molding, a rim, rings, and poles for carrying. Gold dishes, pans, pitchers, and bowls were to be made for it. The Bread of the Presence was to be set out on the table before God at all times.

Then came the lampstand. It was to be made of pure gold, hammered out: base and shaft, cups, buds, and blossoms all of one piece. Six branches were to extend from its sides, three on one side and three on the other, with almond blossoms on the branches. Seven lamps were to be set upon it so they lit the space in front of it. Its wick trimmers and trays were to be of pure gold. A talent of pure gold was to be used for the lampstand and all these accessories. Moses was warned again: see that he made them according to the pattern shown him on the mountain.

From furniture God moved to the tent itself. The tabernacle was to have ten curtains of finely twisted linen and blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, with cherubim worked into them by a skilled craftsman. The curtains would be joined in two sets of five. Loops and gold clasps would hold them together so the tabernacle would be a unit.

Over those curtains would go a tent of goat hair, eleven curtains in all. Over that would go a covering of ram skins dyed red, and over that a covering of durable leather. Around the dwelling would stand upright frames of acacia wood, held by silver bases and crossbars overlaid with gold. The tabernacle was to be set up according to the plan shown to Moses on the mountain.

Inside the tent, a veil mattered more than the walls. It was to be made of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and finely twisted linen, with cherubim worked into it by a skilled craftsman. It would hang on gold hooks set on acacia posts overlaid with gold and standing on silver bases. The veil was to separate the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place. The ark of the testimony was to be placed behind it, and the mercy seat was to be set on top of the ark in the Most Holy Place. The table was to stand outside the veil on the north side, and the lampstand opposite it on the south side. For the entrance to the tent, a curtain of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and finely twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer, would hang on five posts with gold hooks and bronze bases.

Outside, an altar waited. It was to be made of acacia wood, square, five cubits long and five cubits wide and three cubits high. Horns were to be made on its four corners, of one piece with the altar, and it was to be overlaid with bronze. Its pots for removing ashes, shovels, basins for sprinkling, meat forks, and firepans were to be bronze. A bronze grating would sit inside it, with bronze rings and poles for carrying. The altar was to be hollow, made with planks, just as Moses was shown on the mountain.

Around the tabernacle would stand a courtyard, a hundred cubits long and fifty cubits wide, with curtains of finely twisted linen on posts with bronze bases and silver hooks and bands. The entrance curtain would be twenty cubits wide, worked in the same blue, purple, and scarlet pattern. Clear oil from pressed olives was to be brought so the lamps could burn continually. In the tent of meeting, outside the veil that was before the testimony, Aaron and his sons were to tend the lamps before the LORD from evening until morning. This was to be a lasting ordinance for the generations to come.

Then God set apart the men who would serve at the altar.

Moses was to bring Aaron and his sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar to the entrance of the tent of meeting and wash them with water. Sacred garments were to be made for Aaron, for glory and for beauty. God named their parts in detail: a breastpiece, an ephod, a robe, a woven tunic, a turban, and a sash. Wise men, filled by God with a spirit of wisdom, were to make them so Aaron could be consecrated to serve as priest.

The ephod was to be made of gold and blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and finely twisted linen, the work of a skilled craftsman. Its shoulder pieces would carry two onyx stones set in gold filigree, engraved with the names of the sons of Israel, six on one stone and six on the other, in the order of their birth. Aaron would bear their names on his shoulders before the LORD as a memorial.

The breastpiece of judgment was to be made in the same colors and workmanship. It would be square and folded double. Four rows of precious stones would be mounted on it, three in each row, twelve stones in all, each engraved like a seal with the name of one of the twelve tribes. It would be attached to the ephod and worn over Aaron's heart whenever he entered the Holy Place. Aaron would bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment on his heart before the LORD continually. The Urim and the Thummim were also to be put in the breastpiece, so Aaron would bear the means of making decisions for the Israelites over his heart before the LORD.

The robe of the ephod was to be made entirely of blue cloth. Around its hem were to be pomegranates of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, with gold bells between them. The sound of the bells would be heard when Aaron entered the Holy Place before the LORD and when he came out, so that he would not die. A plate of pure gold was to be made and engraved like a seal: HOLY TO THE LORD. It would be fastened with a blue cord to the front of the turban. Aaron would wear it on his forehead continually, bearing the guilt connected with the sacred gifts Israel offered, so they might be acceptable before the LORD.

The consecration of Aaron and his sons would take seven days. A young bull and two rams without defect were to be brought. Unleavened bread and cakes and wafers without yeast would be placed in a basket. Aaron and his sons would be washed, dressed, and anointed. The bull would be slaughtered as a sin offering. Blood would be put on the horns of the altar and poured out at its base. One ram would be offered whole as a burnt offering. The other ram, the ram of ordination, would be slaughtered, and its blood put on the tip of Aaron's right ear, on the tips of his sons' right ears, on the thumbs of their right hands, and on the big toes of their right feet, with blood splashed against the sides of the altar. Anointing oil and altar blood would be sprinkled on Aaron and his garments, and on his sons and their garments. They would be consecrated. Portions of the ordination ram and bread would be waved before the LORD and eaten by Aaron and his sons at the entrance of the tent of meeting. For seven days the process would be repeated, and the altar would be made holy through atonement.

Every day after that, two lambs a year old were to be offered regularly. One lamb would be offered in the morning and the other at twilight, with flour mixed with oil and a drink offering of wine. This burnt offering would stand at the entrance of the tent of meeting before the LORD, where God promised to meet His people and speak to Moses. The place would be consecrated by God's glory.

"Then I will dwell among the Israelites and be their God. And they will know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out of the land of Egypt so that I might dwell among them. I am the LORD their God."

God planned more furniture and more fragrance. The altar of incense was to be made of acacia wood overlaid with pure gold, with a horn at each corner, rings for poles, and a place before the veil that was before the ark of the testimony. Aaron was to burn fragrant incense on it every morning when he tended the lamps and again at twilight when he lit them, so incense would burn regularly before the LORD for generations. No unauthorized incense was to be offered there, and no burnt offering, grain offering, or drink offering was to be poured on it. Once a year Aaron was to make atonement on its horns with blood from the sin offering of atonement. It was most holy to the LORD.

When a census was taken, each man counted was to give a ransom for his life to the LORD, half a shekel according to the sanctuary shekel. Rich and poor would give the same amount. The atonement money from the Israelites was to be appointed for the service of the tent of meeting, as a memorial before the LORD. A bronze basin on a bronze stand was to be placed between the tent of meeting and the altar. Aaron and his sons were to wash their hands and feet with water from it whenever they entered the tent of meeting or approached the altar to minister, so they would not die.

The anointing oil itself was holy. It would be mixed by a skilled perfumer from liquid myrrh, cinnamon, fragrant cane, cassia, and olive oil, according to exact amounts. With it Moses was to anoint the tent of meeting, the ark of the testimony, the table and all its articles, the lampstand and its accessories, the altar of incense, the altar of burnt offering and all its utensils, and the basin with its stand. Whatever touched them would become holy. Aaron and his sons were to be anointed and consecrated to serve as priests. The oil was not to be poured on anyone else's body, and Israel was not to make any like it for common use. It was sacred. Incense too was given a pure formula of spices, blended by a perfumer, salted, pure, and sacred. It was not to be made for private use. Whoever made any like it to enjoy its fragrance was to be cut off from his people.

God then named the chief craftsman. He had chosen Bezalel, son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. "And I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with skill, ability, and knowledge in all kinds of craftsmanship," to make artistic designs in gold, silver, and bronze, to cut and set stones, to work in wood, and to engage in all kinds of crafts. God also appointed Oholiab, son of Ahisamak, of the tribe of Dan, to help him. And God gave ability to all the skilled workers, so they would make everything commanded: the tent of meeting, the ark of the testimony with its mercy seat, all the furnishings, the table and its articles, the pure gold lampstand and all its accessories, the altar of incense, the altar of burnt offering and all its utensils, the basin with its stand, the woven garments for Aaron and his sons, the anointing oil, and the fragrant incense for the Holy Place. They were to make them just as God commanded.

Above all the skill still stood the Sabbath. The Israelites were to observe the Sabbath throughout their generations as a lasting covenant. It was a sign between God and Israel forever, for in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, and on the seventh day He rested and was refreshed. Anyone who desecrated the Sabbath was to be put to death. Anyone who did any work on it was to be cut off from among his people. Six days work could be done, but the seventh was a day of Sabbath rest, holy to the LORD.

"When the LORD had finished speaking with Moses on Mount Sinai, He gave him the two tablets of the Testimony, tablets of stone inscribed by the finger of God."

Moses held God's words on stone. Below the mountain, the camp was still waiting, and the waiting was about to turn.

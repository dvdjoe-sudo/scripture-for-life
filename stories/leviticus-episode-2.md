Leviticus Episode 2: The Priests Begin, and Fire Falls Twice
Leviticus 8 to 10, Berean Standard Bible

The laws had named Aaron and his sons for seven chapters. Now the whole congregation was called to watch them become what the laws had called them.

The LORD told Moses to take Aaron and his sons, their garments, the anointing oil, the bull of the sin offering, the two rams, and the basket of unleavened bread.

"And assemble the whole congregation at the entrance to the Tent of Meeting."

Moses did as the LORD had commanded him, and the assembly gathered. His first words to them set the ground for everything that followed.

"This is what the LORD has commanded to be done."

Moses presented Aaron and his sons and washed them with water. Then he dressed Aaron piece by piece. He put the tunic on him and tied the sash around him. He clothed him with the robe and put the ephod on him, fastened with its woven band. He put the breastpiece on him and placed the Urim and the Thummim in the breastpiece. He put the turban on Aaron's head and set the gold plate, the holy diadem, on the front of the turban, as the LORD had commanded him.

Then Moses took the anointing oil and anointed the tabernacle and everything in it, and so he consecrated them. He sprinkled some of the oil on the altar seven times, anointing the altar and all its utensils, and the basin with its stand, to consecrate them. He poured some of the anointing oil on Aaron's head and anointed him to consecrate him. Aaron's sons were presented next, more plainly dressed than their father: tunics put on them, sashes wrapped around them, headbands tied on them, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

The offerings began, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on the head of each animal before Moses slaughtered it.

First came the bull for the sin offering. Moses took some of its blood and applied it with his finger to all four horns of the altar, purifying the altar. He poured out the rest of the blood at the base of the altar and consecrated it so that atonement could be made on it. The fat on the entrails, the lobe of the liver, and both kidneys with their fat were burned on the altar. The rest of the bull, its hide, flesh, and dung, he burned outside the camp, as the LORD had commanded him.

Then came the ram for the burnt offering, cut into pieces and burned whole on the altar, head, pieces, and fat, its entrails and legs washed with water, a burnt offering, a pleasing aroma, a food offering to the LORD.

Then came the other ram, the ram of ordination, and this one marked the men themselves. Moses slaughtered it and took some of its blood and put it on Aaron's right earlobe, on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot. He presented Aaron's sons and did the same for each of them: right earlobe, right thumb, right big toe. Then he splattered the rest of the blood on all sides of the altar.

Moses took the fat of that ram, its fat tail, the fat on the entrails, the lobe of the liver, both kidneys with their fat, and the right thigh. From the basket of unleavened bread that was before the LORD he took one cake of unleavened bread, one cake of bread made with oil, and one wafer, and placed them on the fat portions and on the right thigh. He put all of it in the hands of Aaron and his sons, and they waved it before the LORD as a wave offering. Then Moses took it from their hands and burned it on the altar with the burnt offering. The breast of the ram was waved before the LORD as Moses' own portion.

Last, Moses took some of the anointing oil and some of the blood that was on the altar and sprinkled them on Aaron and his garments, and on his sons and their garments. So he consecrated Aaron and his garments, as well as Aaron's sons and their garments.

The new priests did not go home that night. Moses told them to boil the meat at the entrance to the Tent of Meeting and eat it there with the bread in the basket of ordination offerings, and to burn up whatever was left of the meat and bread.

"You must not go outside the entrance to the Tent of Meeting for seven days, until the days of your ordination are complete; for it will take seven days to ordain you."

They were to remain at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting day and night for seven days and keep the LORD's charge, so that they would not die. The chapter ends with the sentence the book keeps aiming at.

"So Aaron and his sons did everything the LORD had commanded through Moses."

On the eighth day, Moses summoned Aaron and his sons and the elders of Israel. Seven days of ordination were complete. Now Aaron would do, for the first time, the work he had watched Moses do.

Moses told him to take a young bull for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, both without blemish, for himself. For the people, there was to be a male goat for a sin offering, a calf and a lamb, both a year old and without blemish, for a burnt offering, an ox and a ram for a peace offering, and a grain offering mixed with oil.

"For today the LORD will appear to you."

They brought what Moses had commanded to the front of the Tent of Meeting, and the whole congregation drew near and stood before the LORD.

"This is what the LORD has commanded you to do, so that the glory of the LORD may appear to you."

Then Moses turned to his brother with an order that had been building since the burning bush.

"Approach the altar and sacrifice your sin offering and your burnt offering to make atonement for yourself and for the people. And sacrifice the people's offering to make atonement for them, as the LORD has commanded."

Aaron approached the altar. He slaughtered the calf as a sin offering for himself. His sons brought the blood to him, and he dipped his finger in it and applied it to the horns of the altar and poured out the rest at the base. He burned the fat, the kidneys, and the lobe of the liver on the altar, as the LORD had commanded Moses, and burned the flesh and the hide outside the camp.

Then he slaughtered the burnt offering. His sons brought him the blood, and he splattered it on all sides of the altar. They brought him the offering piece by piece, including the head, and he burned the pieces on the altar, with the entrails and legs washed and burned on top. Then he presented the people's offering: the male goat for their sin offering, slaughtered and offered for sin like the first one; the burnt offering, offered according to the ordinance; the grain offering, a handful of it burned on the altar in addition to the morning's burnt offering; and the ox and the ram of the peace offering, their blood splattered on the altar, their fat portions placed on the breasts and burned, while the breasts and the right thigh were waved before the LORD, as Moses had commanded.

The work was done. Aaron lifted up his hands toward the people and blessed them, and having made the sin offering, the burnt offering, and the peace offering, he stepped down.

Moses and Aaron then entered the Tent of Meeting. When they came out, they blessed the people, and the glory of the LORD appeared to all the people.

"Fire came out from the presence of the LORD and consumed the burnt offering and the fat portions on the altar. And when all the people saw it, they shouted for joy and fell facedown."

That shout is the high point of the book so far. The tent that Moses could not enter at the end of Exodus had a fire burning on its altar now, lit from the presence of the LORD Himself. The priests were ordained. The offerings worked. The people were on their faces, and the sound they made was joy.

The next verse opens with a word that changes the temperature of the whole story: now.

"Now Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu took their censers, put fire in them and added incense, and offered unauthorized fire before the LORD, contrary to His command."

Nadab and Abihu were Aaron's two oldest sons. They had been washed and dressed in this same courtyard. Blood from the ram of ordination had been put on their ears, their thumbs, and their toes. Anointing oil and altar blood had been sprinkled on their garments. They stood closer to the work of the tent than anyone in Israel except their father.

The text does not say why they did it. It says what they did. They took their censers, put fire in them and added incense, and offered it before the LORD. The fire was unauthorized. It was contrary to His command.

"So fire came out from the presence of the LORD and consumed them, and they died in the presence of the LORD."

The same fire, from the same presence, in the same place. On the altar it had taken the offering and the people had shouted. In the censers of Nadab and Abihu it took the two men carrying them.

Moses spoke to Aaron at once, and what he gave him was not comfort. It was an explanation, in God's own earlier words.

"To those who come near Me I will show My holiness, and in the sight of all the people I will reveal My glory."

But Aaron remained silent.

Moses summoned Mishael and Elzaphan, sons of Aaron's uncle Uzziel, and told them to come and carry the bodies of their cousins outside the camp, away from the front of the sanctuary. They came forward and carried them, still in their tunics, outside the camp, as Moses had directed.

Then Moses spoke to Aaron and to the two sons who remained, Eleazar and Ithamar. They were not to let their hair become disheveled and not to tear their garments, the ordinary signs of mourning, or they would die and the LORD would be angry with the whole congregation. Their brothers, the whole house of Israel, could mourn for the burning the LORD had kindled. Aaron and his remaining sons could not. They were not to go outside the entrance to the Tent of Meeting, or they would die, for the LORD's anointing oil was on them. They did as Moses instructed. The anointing oil was on them, and their place at the entrance of the tent did not release them to mourn the way the rest of Israel could.

Then, for the only time in these chapters, the LORD spoke to Aaron directly, and the word He gave the grieving father was a rule for the tent.

"You and your sons are not to drink wine or strong drink when you enter the Tent of Meeting, or else you will die; this is a permanent statute for the generations to come."

The reason followed the rule. The priests had to distinguish between the holy and the common, between the clean and the unclean, so that they could teach the Israelites all the statutes the LORD had given them through Moses. The men set apart to draw the line for the whole people had to keep their own heads clear when they crossed into the tent.

The work of the altar did not pause for the funeral. Moses told Aaron, Eleazar, and Ithamar to take the grain offering that remained from the food offerings to the LORD and eat it without leaven beside the altar, because it was most holy, their share and their sons' share. The breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the contribution could be eaten in a clean place by them and their sons and daughters, because those portions had been assigned to them from the peace offerings of the people.

Then Moses searched carefully for the goat of the sin offering, and found that it had been burned up. He was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron's remaining sons. The sin offering was most holy. It had been given to them to take away the guilt of the congregation by making atonement for them before the LORD. Its blood had not been brought inside the holy place, so they should have eaten it in the sanctuary area, as he had commanded. Why had they not eaten it?

Aaron answered this time. The silence that had held since his sons died broke on a question about a meal, and what came out was the only speech Aaron makes in the whole episode.

"Behold, this very day they presented their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD. Since these things have happened to me, if I had eaten the sin offering today, would it have been acceptable in the sight of the LORD?"

He did not defend his sons. He did not argue the rule. He named the day: this very day, the offerings presented, and these things that had happened to him. Two of his sons were dead outside the camp. Two remained, and they had burned the goat instead of eating it. Would eating it, on this day, have been acceptable in the sight of the LORD?

Moses heard the explanation, and Moses was satisfied. The chapter ends there, with no further word from God on the matter recorded, and no punishment falling on Eleazar and Ithamar.

So the priesthood of Israel began with both kinds of fire in a single day. Fire from the LORD consumed the offering, and the congregation shouted and fell on their faces. Fire from the LORD consumed the high priest's two oldest sons, and their father stood in his robes and said nothing. Before the day was over the same father had to answer for a goat his surviving sons had burned instead of eaten, and his answer was accepted.

Aaron still wore the gold plate on his turban. Eleazar and Ithamar still stood beside him at the altar. The fire on that altar was still burning, and by the command given in the last episode, it was not to be extinguished.

What the priests now had to learn, and to teach everyone else, was where the lines ran: holy and common, clean and unclean, what could come near the tent and what had to be carried outside the camp. That teaching starts with the food on Israel's tables and the marks on Israel's skin.

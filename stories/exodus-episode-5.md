Exodus Episode 5: The Mountain and the Covenant
Exodus 19 to 24, Berean Standard Bible

On the first day of the third month after the Israelites left Egypt, they entered the Desert of Sinai and camped in front of the mountain. The runaway shepherd who had once led Jethro's flock to Horeb now stood at that same mountain with a nation at his back.

Moses went up to God. The LORD called to him from the mountain and told him what to say to the house of Jacob and the people of Israel. They had seen what God did to the Egyptians. They had seen how He carried them on eagles' wings and brought them to Himself. "Now if you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant, you will be My treasured possession out of all the nations, for the whole earth is Mine. And unto Me you shall be a kingdom of priests and a holy nation."

Moses came down and summoned the elders. He set before them all the words the LORD had commanded him. "And all the people answered together, 'We will do everything that the LORD has spoken.'" Moses brought their words back to the LORD.

The LORD told Moses He would come to him in a dense cloud, so the people would hear Him speaking with Moses and would always trust him. Moses was to consecrate the people that day and the next. They were to wash their clothes and be ready by the third day, because on the third day the LORD would come down on Mount Sinai in the sight of all the people.

Boundaries were set around the mountain. The people were not to go up the mountain or touch its edge. Whoever touched the mountain was to be put to death. No hand was to touch that person. He was to be stoned or shot with arrows, whether man or animal. He was not to live. Only when the ram's horn sounded a long blast could the people approach the mountain.

Moses consecrated the people. They washed their clothes. He told them to be ready by the third day and not to go near a woman.

"On the third day, when morning came, there was thunder and lightning. A thick cloud was upon the mountain, and a very loud blast of the ram's horn went out, so that all the people in the camp trembled." Moses brought the people out of the camp to meet God, and they took their stand at the foot of the mountain. Mount Sinai was covered with smoke because the LORD descended on it in fire. The smoke billowed up like smoke from a furnace, and the whole mountain trembled violently. The sound of the trumpet grew louder and louder. Moses spoke, and God answered him in thunder.

The LORD descended to the top of Mount Sinai and called Moses up. Moses went up. The LORD warned him to go down again and warn the people not to force their way through to see the LORD, or many of them would perish. Even the priests, who approached the LORD, had to consecrate themselves, or the LORD would break out against them. Moses answered that the people could not come up because God Himself had warned them to consecrate the mountain and set its boundaries. The LORD told him to go down and bring Aaron up with him, but not to let the priests and people break through. Moses went down and told the people.

Then God spoke the words Israel would carry for the rest of its life.

"I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. You shall have no other gods before Me."

They were not to make an idol for themselves, in the form of anything in heaven above, on the earth below, or in the waters beneath the earth. They were not to bow down to idols or worship them, for the LORD their God is a jealous God, punishing the children for the sin of the parents to the third and fourth generation of those who hate Him, but showing love to a thousand generations of those who love Him and keep His commandments.

"You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain, for the LORD will not leave anyone unpunished who takes His name in vain."

"Remember the Sabbath day by keeping it holy." Six days they were to labor and do all their work. The seventh day was a Sabbath to the LORD their God. On it they were not to do any work, neither they, nor sons or daughters, nor male or female servants, nor animals, nor any foreigner residing in their towns. In six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, but He rested on the seventh day. Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy.

"Honor your father and mother, so that your days may be long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you."

"You shall not murder."

"You shall not commit adultery."

"You shall not steal."

"You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor."

"You shall not covet your neighbor's house. You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, or his manservant or maidservant, or his ox or donkey, or anything that belongs to your neighbor."

When the people saw the thunder and lightning, heard the trumpet, and saw the mountain in smoke, they trembled with fear and stayed at a distance. They said to Moses that he should speak to them, and they would listen. They did not want God to speak to them, or they would die. Moses told them not to be afraid. God had come to test them, so that the fear of God would be with them to keep them from sinning. The people remained at a distance while Moses approached the thick darkness where God was.

The laws that followed filled the space between God's mountain and Israel's daily life. They covered altars and idols, servants and violence, injury and repayment, theft and lost property, loans and pledges, the stranger, the widow, the orphan, the poor, and the rest owed to people, animals, and land.

Some laws were hard. A Hebrew servant bought by an Israelite was to serve six years and go free in the seventh without paying anything. Rules governed a daughter sold as a servant, a wife's food, clothing, and marital rights, and what happened when masters injured the people under their power. A man who willfully attacked another and killed him was to be put to death, but a man whose killing was not premeditated could flee to a place God appointed. A man who struck his father or mother was to be put to death. A kidnapper was to be put to death. Anyone who cursed father or mother was to be put to death. Injuries were to be measured and repaid with care: life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise. An owner whose ox gored a man or woman to death faced judgment, and the ox was to be stoned.

Other laws reached into the ordinary corners of the camp. A thief caught breaking in had different consequences by night and by day. A man who stole an ox or sheep and slaughtered or sold it had to pay back five cattle for the ox and four sheep for the sheep. A man who borrowed an animal answered for it under set terms. A man who seduced a virgin who was not pledged in marriage had to marry her and pay the bride price, unless her father refused to give her to him, and then he still had to pay. A sorceress was not to be allowed to live. Anyone who had sexual relations with an animal was to be put to death. Anyone who sacrificed to any god other than the LORD was to be destroyed.

God also bound Israel's memory to its mercy. "You must not exploit or oppress a foreign resident, for you yourselves were foreigners in the land of Egypt." "You must not mistreat any widow or orphan." If a widow or orphan cried out to God, God would hear their cry. "If you lend money to one of My people among you who is poor, you must not act as a creditor to him; you are not to charge him interest." If a neighbor's cloak was taken as a pledge, it was to be returned by sunset, because the cloak was the only covering he had for his body. If he cried out to God, God would hear, for God is compassionate.

Justice had rules too. The Israelites were not to spread false reports or help a guilty man by being a malicious witness. They were not to follow the crowd in doing wrong or pervert justice by siding with the crowd. They were not to show favoritism to a poor man in his lawsuit or deny justice to a poor man because he was poor. They were to keep far from false charges and not kill the innocent or righteous. They were not to accept a bribe, for a bribe blinds those who see and twists the words of the innocent. Even an enemy's ox or donkey wandering away was to be taken back. Even an enemy's animal fallen under its load was not to be left without help.

Rest ran through the laws as well as through the week. For six years Israel sowed the land and gathered its produce. In the seventh year the land was to lie unplowed and unused so the poor among the people could get food from it, and wild animals could eat what was left. The same was to be done with vineyards and olive groves. "For six days you are to do your work, but on the seventh day you must cease, so that your ox and your donkey may rest and the son of your maidservant may be refreshed, as well as the foreign resident."

Three times a year all the men were to appear before the Sovereign LORD: at the Festival of Unleavened Bread, which remembered the month Israel came out of Egypt; at the Festival of Harvest, with the firstfruits of the crops sown in the field; and at the Festival of Ingathering at the end of the year, when the crops were gathered from the field.

God promised to send an angel ahead of Israel to guard them along the way and bring them to the place He had prepared. Israel was to pay attention to him and listen to what he said. They were not to rebel against him. If Israel obeyed God's voice and did all He said, God would be an enemy to their enemies and oppose those who opposed them. They were not to bow down to the gods of the nations ahead or serve them. They were to serve the LORD their God, and He would bless their bread and water and take sickness away from them. God would drive out the nations little by little, not in a single year, lest the land become desolate and the wild animals become too many. He would set Israel's borders from the Red Sea to the Sea of the Philistines, and from the desert to the Euphrates River.

After Moses gave the people these words, he went and told them all the words and ordinances of the LORD. "They all responded with one voice: 'All the words that the LORD has spoken, we will do.'"

Moses wrote down all the words of the LORD. Early the next morning he got up and built an altar at the foot of the mountain and set up twelve stone pillars representing the twelve tribes of Israel. He sent young Israelite men to offer burnt offerings and sacrifice young bulls as fellowship offerings to the LORD. Moses took half of the blood and put it in basins. The other half he splashed against the altar. Then he took the Book of the Covenant and read it to the people. They responded that all the LORD had said they would do, and they would obey. "So Moses took the blood, splattered it on the people, and said, 'This is the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you in accordance with all these words.'"

Moses and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel went up and saw the God of Israel. "Under His feet was a work like a pavement made of sapphire, as clear as the sky itself." God did not raise His hand against these leaders of the Israelites. They saw God, and they ate and drank.

The LORD told Moses to come up to Him on the mountain and stay there. He would give him the tablets of stone, with the law and commandments He had written for their instruction. Moses set out with Joshua, his aide. He told the elders to wait there until he and Aaron came back. If anyone had a dispute, he could go to Aaron and Hur, who were with the elders.

When Moses went up on the mountain, the cloud covered it. The glory of the LORD settled on Mount Sinai. For six days the cloud covered the mountain. On the seventh day the LORD called to Moses from within the cloud. To the Israelites, the glory of the LORD looked like a consuming fire on top of the mountain. "Moses entered the cloud as he went up on the mountain, and he remained on the mountain forty days and forty nights."

Below the cloud, the camp waited with the blood of the covenant still fresh in its memory. Above the camp, Moses waited inside the cloud for the words God would place in his hands.

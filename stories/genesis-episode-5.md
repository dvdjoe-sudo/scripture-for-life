Genesis Episode 5: The Call of Abraham
Genesis 12 to 17, Berean Standard Bible

The call came to a man whose family had stopped in Haran.

The LORD said to Abram, "Leave your country, your kindred, and your father's household, and go to the land I will show you. I will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you; I will make your name great, so that you will be a blessing. I will bless those who bless you and curse those who curse you; and all the families of the earth will be blessed through you."

Abram went, as the LORD had told him. He was seventy-five years old when he left Haran. He took Sarai his wife, Lot his brother's son, all the possessions they had gathered, and the people they had acquired in Haran. They set out for the land of Canaan, and they arrived there.

The land was not empty. The Canaanites were in the land. Abram traveled through it to the site of Shechem, to the oak of Moreh. There the LORD appeared to Abram and promised the land to his offspring. Abram built an altar there to the LORD, who had appeared to him. He moved to the hill country east of Bethel, pitched his tent with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east, built another altar, and called on the name of the LORD.

Then famine pressed the land. Abram went down to Egypt to live there for a while, because the famine was severe. Before they entered Egypt, Abram asked Sarai to say she was his sister. He knew her beauty could put his life in danger. Pharaoh's officials saw Sarai, praised her to Pharaoh, and she was taken into Pharaoh's house. Abram was treated well because of her and gained sheep, cattle, donkeys, servants, and camels.

The LORD struck Pharaoh and his household with serious diseases because of Sarai, Abram's wife. Pharaoh sent for Abram, confronted him for the lie, and gave Sarai back. Pharaoh ordered his men to send Abram away with his wife and everything he had.

Back in the Negev, the promise had a practical problem. Abram was wealthy in livestock, silver, and gold. Lot also had flocks, herds, and tents. The land could not support them while they stayed together, and quarrels broke out between Abram's herdsmen and Lot's herdsmen. Abram let Lot choose first. Lot looked toward the plain of the Jordan, saw that it was well watered everywhere, like the garden of the LORD and like Egypt, and chose it. He moved his tents as far as Sodom. The men of Sodom were wicked, sinning greatly against the LORD.

After Lot separated, the LORD told Abram to lift his eyes north, south, east, and west. All the land he saw would be given to him and his offspring forever. His offspring would be like the dust of the earth. Abram moved his tent to the oaks of Mamre at Hebron and built an altar there.

Word soon came that war had swept the Jordan plain. Kings had raided Sodom and Gomorrah and carried off Lot and his possessions. A man who escaped told Abram the Hebrew. Abram brought out 318 trained men born in his household and pursued the raiders as far as Dan. At night he divided his forces, attacked, and recovered Lot, the women, the people, and the goods.

On the way back, Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, brought out bread and wine and blessed Abram. Abram gave him a tenth of everything. The king of Sodom offered Abram the goods if Abram would give him the people. Abram refused to take so much as a thread or a sandal strap from the king of Sodom. He would not let that king say he had made Abram rich.

After these things, the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision. God told him not to fear. God was his shield and his very great reward. Abram answered with the fact that had followed him from Haran. He remained childless. A servant in his house, Eliezer of Damascus, stood to be his heir.

God took Abram outside and told him to count the stars if he could. So would his offspring be.

"Abram believed the LORD, and it was credited to him as righteousness."

God told Abram to bring animals for a covenant: a heifer, a goat, a ram, a turtledove, and a young pigeon. Abram cut the animals in two and laid the pieces opposite each other. Birds of prey came down, and Abram drove them away. As the sun set, a deep sleep fell on Abram, and a thick and dreadful darkness came over him. In that darkness God told him his descendants would be strangers in a land not their own, enslaved and mistreated for four hundred years. God would punish the nation they served, and afterward they would come out with great possessions. In the fourth generation they would return to Canaan, because the sin of the Amorites had not yet reached its full measure.

When darkness had fallen, a smoking firepot and a blazing torch passed between the pieces. On that day the LORD made a covenant with Abram and named the land from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates.

Years passed. Sarai still had no child. She gave Abram her Egyptian slave Hagar and told him to sleep with her, so that Sarai might build a family through her. Abram listened. Hagar conceived, and when she knew she was pregnant, she began to despise her mistress. Sarai blamed Abram. Abram put Hagar back under Sarai's authority, and Sarai treated her harshly until Hagar fled into the wilderness.

The angel of the LORD found Hagar near a spring. He sent her back to Sarai and told her the child would be a son named Ishmael, because the LORD had heard her misery. Ishmael would be a wild donkey of a man, his hand against everyone and everyone's hand against him. Hagar bore Abram a son. Abram was eighty-six years old.

Thirteen years later, when Abram was ninety-nine, the LORD appeared to him as God Almighty. The covenant came with a new name. "No longer will you be called Abram, but your name will be Abraham, for I have made you a father of many nations."

God gave circumcision as the sign of the covenant. Every male in Abraham's household was to be circumcised, including those born in his house and those bought with money. Sarai received a new name too. She would be Sarah, and God would bless her and give Abraham a son by her. Kings of peoples would come from her.

Abraham fell facedown and laughed. A son born to a man one hundred years old and to Sarah at ninety sounded impossible inside ordinary life. Abraham asked that Ishmael might live before God. God answered that Sarah would bear a son, and Abraham was to name him Isaac. God would establish His covenant with Isaac. Ishmael would also be blessed and made into a great nation, but the covenant would stand with Isaac, whom Sarah would bear at the appointed time next year.

That very day Abraham took Ishmael and every male in his household and circumcised them, as God had told him.

Abraham had a new name. Sarah had a new name. Isaac had a name before he had been conceived.

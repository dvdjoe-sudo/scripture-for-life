Joshua Episode 5: The Land Divided, and Caleb's Mountain
Joshua 13 to 15, Berean Standard Bible

The second half of Joshua opens with age and unfinished business in the same sentence. Joshua was old and well along in years, and the LORD said to him: "You are old and well along in years, but very much of the land remains to be possessed." Then God Himself recites the remainder: all the territory of the Philistines and the Geshurites, from the Shihor east of Egypt to Ekron in the north, the five Philistine rulers of Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron, the Avvites, the Canaanite coast up to Aphek, the Gebalites, and all Lebanon eastward to Lebo-hamath, and every Sidonian in the hill country. Over that unconquered map God laid a promise and a procedure: "All the inhabitants of the hill country from Lebanon to Misrephoth-maim", He said, "all the Sidonians", "I Myself will drive out before the Israelites. Be sure to divide it by lot as an inheritance to Israel, as I have commanded you." Divide land you do not yet hold, to nine tribes and the half-tribe of Manasseh. The allotment was an act of war by other means, a deed drawn up in advance of the battles.

Before the new lots, the book re-surveys the old ones. East of the Jordan, Moses had already given the inheritance: Reuben on the plateau from Aroer on the Arnon through Heshbon's cities to the Jordan bank, Gad in Gilead and the valley towns up to the Sea of Chinnereth, the half-tribe of Manasseh through all of Bashan with its sixty cities of Jair. Two honest marks sit inside the survey. One is a name in the casualty list: "The Israelites also killed the diviner Balaam son of Beor along with the others they put to the sword", the prophet of Numbers who blessed Israel four times and died in Midian's camp anyway. The other is a failure that never got fixed: "but the Israelites did not drive out the Geshurites or the Maacathites. So Geshur and Maacath dwell among the Israelites to this day." And twice, once for Moses' grants and once for the whole system, the survey repeats the exception at its center: "To the tribe of Levi, however, Moses had given no inheritance. The LORD, the God of Israel, is their inheritance, just as He had promised them."

The dividing west of the Jordan was done by Eleazar the priest, Joshua, and the heads of the tribes, by lot, exactly as commanded. Joseph's line had become two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim, keeping the count at twelve inheritances with Levi landless. And before the first lot was cast, an old man walked up to Joshua at Gilgal with a claim older than the conquest.

Caleb son of Jephunneh had waited forty-five years to make this speech, and the text gives it to him nearly whole. "You know what the LORD said to Moses the man of God at Kadesh-barnea about you and me. I was forty years old when Moses the servant of the LORD sent me from Kadesh-barnea to spy out the land, and I brought back to him an honest report. Although my brothers who went with me made the hearts of the people melt with fear, I remained loyal to the LORD my God." The melting word again, the word that had traveled from the spies to Jericho to Ai, and Caleb names it as the thing he had refused to do. Then the oath: "On that day Moses swore to me, saying, 'Surely the land on which you have set foot will be an inheritance to you and your children forever, because you have wholly followed the LORD my God.'"

"Now behold, as the LORD promised, He has kept me alive these forty-five years since He spoke this word to Moses, while Israel wandered in the wilderness. So here I am today, eighty-five years old, still as strong today as I was the day Moses sent me out. As my strength was then, so it is now for war, for going out, and for coming in."

Then the ask, and it is not for rest or for a quiet valley: "Now therefore give me this hill country that the LORD promised me on that day, for you yourself heard then that the Anakim were there, with great and fortified cities. Perhaps with the LORD's help I will drive them out, as the LORD has spoken." The giants whose report had killed a generation were Caleb's chosen inheritance. Joshua blessed him and gave him Hebron, and the narrator writes the verdict the whole book has been building toward for this one man: "Hebron belongs to Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite as an inheritance to this day, because he wholly followed the LORD, the God of Israel." The chapter ends with the refrain: "Then the land had rest from war."

Judah's lot came first and largest, borders drawn from the southern tip of the Salt Sea along Edom's edge to the Brook of Egypt, up the Jordan mouth past the Valley of Achor and Gilgal, along the southern slope of Jebusite Jerusalem, and west to the Great Sea. Inside that allotment Caleb collected his promise personally. He drove the three sons of Anak out of Hebron, Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, the children of Anak himself, and then marched on Debir and offered his daughter Achsah in marriage to the man who took it. His nephew Othniel took it. Achsah then rode to her father with a request of her own, dismounted, and asked straight: "Give me a blessing". "Since you have given me land in the Negev, give me springs of water as well." Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs. In a book of borders, she is the one person who negotiates better land terms and gets them.

Judah's towns were counted by district, twenty-nine in the Negev, then the foothills in runs of fourteen, sixteen, and nine, Ekron and Ashdod and Gaza on the coast, the hill country in runs of eleven, nine, ten, six, and two, and six in the wilderness down to En-gedi, more than a hundred cities and villages in all. But the allotment's last line is the honest one, and it hangs over everything the lots will draw in the next chapters: "But the descendants of Judah could not drive out the Jebusites living in Jerusalem. So to this day the Jebusites live there among the descendants of Judah." The greatest tribe held the deed to Jerusalem, and foreigners still lived in it. The dividing of the land and the possessing of it were going to be two different stories.

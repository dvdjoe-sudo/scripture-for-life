2 Kings Episode 7: Jehu's Revolt
2 Kings 9, Berean Standard Bible

The revolt begins in an inner room, with a flask of oil and an order to run. Elisha summoned one of the sons of the prophets and sent him to Ramoth-gilead, where Israel's army was holding the line against Hazael and its commander, Jehu, sat with his fellow officers. The young prophet was to take Jehu aside, pour the oil on his head, deliver the commission, and flee: “I anoint you king over Israel.” The commission he carried was longer than the anointing line. Jehu was to strike down the house of Ahab, “you are to strike down the house of your master Ahab, so that I may avenge the blood of My servants the prophets”, and the blood of all the servants of the LORD shed by Jezebel's hand. The house of Ahab would be made like the houses of Jeroboam and Baasha before it, and its queen's end was named in advance: “on the plot of ground at Jezreel the dogs will devour Jezebel, and there will be no one to bury her.” The young prophet poured the oil and declared, “I anoint you king over the LORD’s people Israel.” Then he opened the door and ran, exactly as instructed. Jehu's fellow officers, told that a madman had come and gone, demanded the message, and when Jehu repeated it, each man stripped off his garment and laid it under Jehu's feet on the bare steps, blew the ram's horn, and proclaimed: “Jehu is king!”

Jehu's first act as king was to seal the camp: no one was to escape to carry the news to Jezreel, where Joram lay recovering from his wounds with Ahaziah of Judah visiting at his side. Then he drove. The watchman on Jezreel's tower saw the company coming and sent a rider to ask the formal question, “Have you come in peace?” Jehu's answer absorbed the messenger into the revolt: “What do you know about peace?” “Fall in behind me.” A second rider got the same words and the same fate, and the watchman, studying the distant chariot, named the driver by his driving: “he is driving like a madman!” Joram and Ahaziah came out to meet him, each in his own chariot, and they met Jehu on the one piece of ground in Israel where this confrontation had to happen: the property of Naboth the Jezreelite, the vineyard Ahab had taken by murder. Joram asked his own question: “Have you come in peace, Jehu?” Jehu answered: “How can there be peace,” “as long as the idolatry and witchcraft of your mother Jezebel abound?” Joram reined around and fled, shouting the alarm to his nephew: “Treachery, Ahaziah!” Jehu's arrow caught him between the shoulders and pierced his heart, and he slumped in his chariot. Jehu ordered the body thrown onto Naboth's plot, and quoted the oracle Elijah had spoken over that field years before: “As surely as I saw the blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons yesterday, declares the LORD, so will I repay you on this plot of ground, declares the LORD.” Ahaziah fled toward Beth-haggan, was shot in his chariot on the Ascent of Gur, and died at Megiddo, and his servants carried him home to burial in Jerusalem.

Then Jehu entered Jezreel, and the queen mother was waiting for him at a window, her eyes painted, her head adorned. Jezebel's last words were a taunt aimed at the history of coups like his: “Have you come in peace, O Zimri, murderer of your master?” Zimri had reigned seven days. Jehu ignored the taunt and addressed the palace staff instead: “Who is on my side? Who?” Two or three eunuchs looked down, and he gave the order: “Throw her down!” Her blood splattered the wall and the horses, and they trampled her underfoot. Jehu went inside and ate and drank, and only then remembered the forms: “Take care of this cursed woman,” he said, “and bury her, for she was the daughter of a king.” But when they went out to bury her, they found nothing left but her skull, her feet, and the palms of her hands. Jehu's verdict on the discovery closes the episode and reaches back two books for its authority: “On the plot of ground at Jezreel the dogs will devour the flesh of Jezebel.” “Jezebel’s body will lie like dung in the field on the plot of ground at Jezreel, so that no one can say: This is Jezebel.” The word Elijah had spoken at Naboth's vineyard, the word that had hung over the house of Ahab through the whole of 1 Kings, was now fully paid, and the man who collected it was just getting started.
